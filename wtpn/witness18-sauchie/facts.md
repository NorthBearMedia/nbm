# WITNESS 18 — The Sauchie poltergeist, Clackmannanshire, Nov–Dec 1960.
# Witness: Margaret Stewart, Virginia Campbell's schoolteacher.

Cinematic Sauchie ran 24 Aug (EP45); witness retell is 14 Sep = 21 days, gap met.
Chosen because the phenomena were witnessed in a CLASSROOM by a teacher, and
independently by a doctor and a minister — an unusually well-attested case.

## Sources
- Psi Encyclopedia (SPR), "Sauchie Poltergeist" — verbatim quotes below
- Spooky Isles, "The Sauchie Poltergeist: From Despair To Hope"
- Scottish Daily Express (2021) on the surviving audio recording
- Malcolm Robinson, "The Sauchie Poltergeist" (case book)

## Facts used (verbatim where quoted)
- Virginia Campbell, ELEVEN years old, youngest child of James and Annie
  Campbell, moved from Ireland in late 1960 to her brother's house in Sauchie,
  Clackmannanshire. She shared a bed with her nine-year-old niece.
- Disturbances ran from 22 November 1960, continuing roughly five weeks.
- LINEN CHEST (verbatim): witnesses saw "a large wooden chest full of bed linen
  rock and levitate slightly, move some eighteen inches across the vinyl floor,
  and then return to its original position".
- PILLOW (verbatim): "Virginia's pillow rotating some 60 degrees, while her head
  was lying on it, in a manner that it would have been impossible for her to do
  herself".
- CLASSROOM — MARGARET STEWART, her teacher, observed (verbatim): "the girl's
  desk lid rise steeply, although she did not appear to be moving it" and "an
  unoccupied desk behind Virginia rise about an inch off the floor".
- DR NISBET, the family doctor, observed a "puckering, as if due to traction by
  an invisible agency" on the bedclothes.
- Rev. T.W. Lund, the local Church of Scotland minister, was called in and
  witnessed phenomena including the linen chest.
- A film camera and tape recorder were set up in Virginia's room by the doctors;
  audio from the case survives.
- Three GPs, ministers and her teacher were all witnesses. No recantation is
  recorded by any of them.

## Dramatised (texture only)
- The teacher's register (a 1960 Scottish primary classroom, register, chalk);
  "she was a shy wee thing, new, and Irish" (documented: shy, well-behaved,
  newly arrived from Ireland); the protective-teacher instinct; interviewer cards.
- The classroom children's reaction is documented in retellings as screaming;
  kept general ("the class went up").

## DO NOT USE
- Sensational "possession" framing — never used by the witnesses.
- Naming Virginia's niece or other children.
- Claiming the case was solved, or that Virginia was later found to be faking.
- Any claim about Virginia's adult life (she was a child; leave her be beyond
  the documented events and the doctors' defence of her).
