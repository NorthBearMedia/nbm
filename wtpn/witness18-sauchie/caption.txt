Sauchie, Clackmannanshire, November 1960 — an eleven-year-old girl had just arrived from Ireland to live at her brother's house. Quiet, well-behaved, third row. Then in front of a full classroom her desk lid rose steeply while her hands were nowhere near it, and an empty desk behind her lifted about an inch off the floor. Her teacher was standing at the front with the chalk in her hand. At the house it was worse: a large wooden chest full of bed linen rocked, lifted, travelled some eighteen inches across the floor and returned; her own doctor watched her pillow rotate roughly sixty degrees while her head was lying on it, in a way she could not have done herself. Three GPs, a Church of Scotland minister and her schoolteacher all witnessed the disturbances. A film camera and a tape recorder were set up in the girl's bedroom, and audio from the case still survives. Her doctor said plainly that the child was not responsible for what was happening. None of them ever took a word of it back. Dramatised from the documented accounts. 🎙️ Twenty children saw it.
