# WITNESS 17 — The Cooneen (Coonian) Ghost, Co. Fermanagh, 1913.
# Witness: Fr Eugene Coyle, parish priest of Maguiresbridge.

The channel ran a CINEMATIC Cooneen episode 20 Aug (EP41). Witness retell is
24 days later (13 Sep) — gap met. Never told as a witness episode before.

## Sources
- Irish News, "Northern Frights at Halloween – The Cooneen Poltergeist" (2015)
- Fermanagh Herald, "The Coonian Ghost house" (2017)
- Unexplained.ie case file (Bridget Murphy / Brookeborough / Canon Eugene Coyle)
- Belfast Entries, "The Cooneen Ghost"; FermanaghRoots wiki
- Contemporary press: the case was reported at the time in the Fermanagh Herald
  and the Impartial Reporter, with eyewitness statements from local clergy and
  councillors. The Murphy family appears in the 1901 Census of Ireland
  (townland of Cornarooslan).

## Facts used
- 1913, a mountain farmhouse at Cooneen near Brookeborough, Co. Fermanagh.
- The household: a widow, Bridget Murphy, and her children. (Counts differ
  across sources — say "her children", never a number.)
- Her husband had died; the disturbances began after his death: knocking at the
  door, heavy footsteps in the loft with nobody there.
- FR EUGENE COYLE, the priest at Maguiresbridge, was asked for help, visited the
  house, and WITNESSED THE PHENOMENA HIMSELF. His described observation: the
  clothes on an empty bed rising and falling, as if someone underneath were
  breathing. (Presented as his description — NOT in quotation marks, as the
  wording varies between retellings.)
- Physical harm to the household: pots and pans thrown; sheets torn off beds;
  drawers shut on the children's fingers; groaning heard.
- Cahir Healy — later a Member of Parliament — also attested to the events.
- The family emigrated to America in 1913.
- The case was covered in the local press at the time; the cottage still stands
  as a ruin and is locally known as the Ghost House.

## Dramatised (texture only)
- Fr Coyle's register as a working country priest ("I was asked to come, the way
  a priest is asked to come"); the parish-duty framing; interiority around being
  a clergyman confronted by something he could not classify; interviewer cards.

## DO NOT USE
- "The ghost followed them to America / onto the ship" — folklore, widely retold,
  NOT established. Excluded entirely.
- Any claim that Fr Coyle was harmed, aged, or died because of the case (a legend
  attached to several such stories).
- Any exorcism outcome or claim the thing was identified or driven out.
- A number for the children (sources conflict).
- Naming individual children.
