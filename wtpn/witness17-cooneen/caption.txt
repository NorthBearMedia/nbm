Cooneen, County Fermanagh, 1913 — a widow living in a mountain farmhouse with her children, her husband lately dead, sent for the parish priest. She told him there was knocking at her door with nobody at it, and footsteps crossing the loft at night. He told her what a priest tells people: that grief plays tricks, that an old house makes noise. Then she told him the rest — pots coming off the walls, sheets pulled off the beds while the children were still in them, and drawers shutting on their fingers in the dark. So he went up the mountain and stayed. What he described seeing has been repeated for over a century: the clothes on an empty bed rising and falling, slowly and regularly, as if someone underneath were breathing. Neighbours came. A man who later sat as an MP put his name to it. The Fermanagh Herald and the Impartial Reporter carried it at the time. The family emigrated to America that year. The house still stands, empty, on the mountain. Dramatised from the documented accounts of the case. 🎙️ Would you have stayed the night?
