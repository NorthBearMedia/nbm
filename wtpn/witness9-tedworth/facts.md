# WITNESS 9 — The Drummer of Tedworth, 1662-63. Witness: John Mompesson, magistrate.

Mompesson's letters were printed almost verbatim by Joseph Glanvill (A Blow at
Modern Sadducism, 1668; Saducismus Triumphatus, 1681). Glanvill visited the
house himself in January 1663 and recorded what he personally heard. The
episode dramatises Mompesson's account, with Glanvill's visit narrated by him.

## Sources
- Burials & Beyond, "The Drummer of Tedworth: Britain's First Poltergeist"
  (verbatim quotes below); The Skeptic; Wikipedia "Drummer of Tedworth";
  Hunter (2005), Historical Research (scholarly treatment exists)
- Pepys diary, Christmas Day 1667: found the account "worth reading indeed"

## Facts used (verbatim where quoted)
- 1661, Ludgershall, Wiltshire: Mompesson (magistrate/excise officer) proceeded
  against William Drury, a vagrant drummer begging with counterfeit passes; the
  bailiff confiscated the drum; it was sent to Mompesson's house in Tedworth.
- From then, ~two years (Mar 1662–Apr 1663+): nocturnal banging, hammering on
  doors and roof; drumming from above the house; after a month it moved
  indoors, centring on the children's room.
- CHILDREN: bedframes beaten; "loud, animalistic scratching" beneath their
  beds; the children shaken and LIFTED in their beds.
- MOMPESSON (verbatim): "It would make Chairs, Tables, Trunks & all moveables
  walk up and down the Rooms."
- Servants lifted/pinned in their beds, covers thrown back; moving lights;
  panting sounds; sulphurous smell; shoes and objects hurled.
- BLACKSMITH's night: "a pair of pincers was continually snapping at the
  blacksmith's nose."
- GLANVILL's OWN VISIT (Jan 1663, verbatim): heard scratching "as loud a
  scratching, as one with long Nails could make upon a Bolster" — the two
  girls' hands visibly above the bedcovers; he searched and found nothing.
- DRURY, in gaol (Gloucester, for theft), to a Wiltshire visitor (verbatim):
  "Ay, says the Drummer, it was because he took my Drum from me… he shall
  never have his quiet again, till I have my Drum, or satisfaction from him."
- Drury prosecuted under the Witchcraft Act 1604; Mompesson and three others
  testified; Drury was transported to the American colonies.
- Mompesson later DENIED IN WRITING the rumour that he had confessed an
  imposture (1672 letter — widely documented); he never withdrew his account.
- Pepys, 25 Dec 1667: "worth reading indeed".

## Dramatised (texture only)
- Magistrate's register ("a court matter, paperwork"); the cost framing (the
  talk that he contrived it — documented complaint of double affliction);
  interviewer cards; ordering.

## DO NOT USE
- The drum being burned (not verified in these sources).
- Royal commission details (not verified here) — omit.
- Red-eyed apparition (not in these sources) — omit.
- Exact trial verdict nuance beyond "tried under the Witchcraft Act;
  transported" (accounts differ on the witchcraft count itself).
