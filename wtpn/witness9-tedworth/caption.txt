Tedworth, Wiltshire, 1662 — a magistrate confiscated a vagrant's drum and sent it home as evidence. That night the banging began, and for two years it did not stop: drumming from the air above the house, his children shaken and lifted in their beds, scratching beneath them "like claws", chairs and tables walking the rooms — his own printed words — lights with no hand to carry them, and a smell of sulphur. A visiting scholar stood in the children's room, their hands in plain view, and heard the scratching himself: "as loud as one with long nails could make upon a bolster." The drummer, in gaol fifty miles away, told a visitor the magistrate would "never have his quiet again" till the drum came back. He was tried under the Witchcraft Act — over a drum — and transported. Samuel Pepys read the printed account on Christmas Day 1667: "worth reading indeed." The magistrate denied any trick, in writing, to his death.
