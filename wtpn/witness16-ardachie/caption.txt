Loch Ness, 1953 — a man bought a hill farm and took the lodge that came with it. Months later he hired a housekeeper, and she began hearing footsteps moving through the house at night. He told her what any employer would: it's an old building, timbers, the cold. Then one night she woke to find an elderly woman crawling slowly along the upstairs corridor on her hands and knees, carrying a candle. By the time anyone else reached her, the corridor was empty — which is usually where a story like this dies. Except the knocking and the footsteps were heard by everyone in that house: the owner, his wife, and both of their staff, awake and listening, night after night, finding nothing. The housekeepers left. The lodge was pulled down years later. But the owner wrote the case up and published it in the Society for Psychical Research's journal under his own name — and he left the doubts in, including that the woman who saw her was excitable, and that nobody could find a single reason for her to lie.
