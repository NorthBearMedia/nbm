# WITNESS 16 — Ardachie Lodge, Loch Ness, 1953. Witness: Peter McEwan, householder.

NEVER COVERED in any format (ledger + live-titles checked). McEwan published the
case HIMSELF in the SPR's Journal (vol. 38, 1955-6) — a householder writing up
his own haunting for a scientific society, doubts included.

## Sources
- SPR: Peter McEwan, "The Ardachie Case", Journal of the SPR 38 (1955-6)
- SPR review of Geoff Holder, "Poltergeist over Scotland" (case summary +
  the SPR's own caveat)
- Spooky Isles "UK's Scariest Ghosts"; Paranormal Database (Highlands);
  multiple case retellings (crawling woman with candle, corridor, everyone
  hearing the knocking)

## Facts used
- December 1952: Peter McEwan and his wife Dorothy took Ardachie Lodge, on the
  shores of Loch Ness, intending to farm the land.
- Months later they advertised for a housekeeper and farmhand; a couple named
  MacDonald answered and moved into the lodge.
- Mrs MacDonald began hearing footsteps moving about the house at night. The
  McEwans reassured her it was an old building settling.
- THE CENTRAL EVENT: late one night Mrs MacDonald woke and saw an elderly woman
  crawling slowly along the upstairs corridor, carrying a candle. The figure was
  gone before anyone else reached her.
- THE KNOCKING AND FOOTSTEPS WERE HEARD BY EVERYONE IN THE HOUSE — not only by
  the woman who saw the apparition. This is the load-bearing fact.
- The figure was linked by the household to a previous occupant of the lodge.
  (Sources spell the name variously — Bruen / Brewin — so DO NOT NAME HER.)
- The MacDonalds did not stay. The lodge was demolished years afterwards.
- McEwan wrote the case up and published it in the SPR Journal.
- THE SPR'S OWN CAVEAT (used honestly on a card): the percipient was noted as
  "clearly excitable" and "certain features of the case arouse suspicion", but
  NO MOTIVE FOR FRAUD was discerned, and the author concluded something
  genuinely paranormal may have occurred.

## Dramatised (texture only)
- McEwan's register as a farmer/academic ("I had bought a hill farm, not a
  ghost story"); the reassure-the-staff beat (documented in substance);
  interviewer cards.
- He narrates Mrs MacDonald's sighting as the householder who took her report
  and investigated it — never claims to have seen the figure himself.

## DO NOT USE
- Naming the previous occupant (spelling/identity unreliable across sources).
- Claiming McEwan saw the apparition — he did not; he heard the knocking.
- The "twelve days" figure and exact demolition date (inconsistent) — say the
  housekeepers did not stay and the lodge was later pulled down.
- Any suggestion Mrs MacDonald was a fraud OR that she was vindicated — quote
  the SPR's actual position: excitable, suspicious features, no motive found.
