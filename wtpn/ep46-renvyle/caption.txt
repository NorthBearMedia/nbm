Renvyle, Connemara, Ireland — the hotel is open tonight, and the reports have never stopped. In 1917 this was the country house of a Dublin surgeon, and his guest was William Butler Yeats — and both men wrote down what happened there. A door that three men could not force, then swinging open on its own. Footsteps crossing empty upstairs rooms. Servants refusing to stay. So Yeats sat a vigil in the haunted room, and his wife saw the thing come through: a pale boy of about fourteen with large luminous eyes — a child of the family who owned Renvyle for three hundred years. His one grievance: strangers in his house. The house burned in 1923; they rebuilt it as a hotel. It did not help. You can book his house for the night — but he told the poet himself what he thinks of strangers. And that is what you are. 👁️ Would you take the room?
