# EP46 — Renvyle House, Connemara — verified facts

CONFIRMED (published first-hand literary accounts — Gogarty and the Yeats circle):
1. Renvyle House, Connemara, County Galway, Ireland — country house on the Atlantic shore,
   owned in the 1910s by Oliver St John Gogarty (surgeon, writer). W. B. Yeats and his wife
   George stayed there (1917 onwards).
2. Disturbances described in PUBLISHED accounts (Gogarty's memoir "As I Was Going Down
   Sackville Street" and Yeats-circle memoirs): heavy footsteps in empty upstairs rooms;
   a door that could not be forced open — pushed back against grown men — and later opened
   freely onto an empty room; rappings; servants refusing to stay. REPORT AS "Gogarty
   wrote" / "the household said".
3. The apparition: during a vigil, the poet's wife George Yeats — a practised medium —
   described a pale boy, about fourteen, with large luminous eyes, materialising in the
   haunted room. The ghost was understood to be a child of the BLAKE family, who had owned
   Renvyle for generations before the Gogartys. Yeats himself led the sitting. ATTRIBUTE:
   "the poet's wife described...", "they understood him to be a Blake."
4. The ghost's communicated grievance (via the sitting): he objected to strangers in the
   house. The stock detail "he appeared, then faded leaving a smell of incense" appears in
   some retellings — SOFT-ATTRIBUTE or OMIT. Safer: he showed himself once, then withdrew.
5. 1923: the house was burned down during the Irish Civil War (by anti-Treaty forces —
   POLITICAL fire; do NOT imply the ghost). Rebuilt c.1930; has operated as the Renvyle
   House Hotel since. The hotel openly tells its ghost history; guests and staff have
   continued to report odd happenings (T1 support — keep wording soft: "reports never
   quite stopped").
6. Witness quality: two of Ireland's most famous literary men left written accounts —
   this is the witness chain to lead with (T3: published first-hand record).

- The fire as paranormal.
