Renvyle, Connemara, Ireland — the surgeon who owned the house watched three grown men fail to push open an unlocked door, onto an empty room. His servants would not sleep upstairs: footsteps in empty rooms, candles quenched as if pinched. His house guest was W.B. Yeats, whose wife sat a vigil in the room and described what came through — a pale boy of about fourteen with great luminous eyes, a child of the family who had held Renvyle for three hundred years. His grievance: strangers in his house. He asked for incense and flowers. In 1923 the house was burned in the Civil War — "they say it took a week to burn" — and rebuilt as a hotel that is open tonight. The owner's account, dramatised from his published memoir and the Yeats papers. 🎙️ Would you take the upstairs room?
