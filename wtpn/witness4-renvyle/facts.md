# WITNESS 4 — Oliver St John Gogarty, Renvyle House, 1917-23

WITNESS: Oliver St John Gogarty — Dublin surgeon, senator, writer — owner of Renvyle
House, Connemara. Published accounts: his memoir "As I Was Going Down Sackville Street"
(1937) + the Yeats-circle record. See also ep46-renvyle/facts.md (same case).

DOCUMENTED: footsteps in empty upstairs rooms; candles suddenly quenched; servants
refusing to stay; the heavy door that could not be forced by grown men then opened
freely onto an empty room (Gogarty's account); W.B. Yeats a guest, his wife George
(medium) sat a vigil/séance; she described a pale boy of about fourteen with large
luminous eyes; understood to be a child of the BLAKE family (owners for ~3 centuries
before); his grievance: strangers in the house; sources record he asked for incense
and flowers. VERBATIM (Gogarty, on the 1923 Civil War burning): "So Renvyle House,
with its irreplaceable oaken panelling, is burned down. They say it took a week to
burn." Rebuilt; reopened 30 April 1930 as Renvyle House Hotel; still operating.

DO NOT USE: the name "Athelstone" (single thin source; keep "a Blake boy"); the fire
as paranormal; invented Gogarty actions (no grave-hunting etc.).
