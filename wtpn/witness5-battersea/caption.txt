Battersea, South London, 1956 — a fifteen-year-old seamstress woke to find a small silver key on her pillow that fitted no lock in the house. Then the knocking started: so loud the neighbours complained, so loud the police brought surveyors in. Nobody ever found a source. Her father, a train driver, told the papers exactly what he saw — his daughter rigid, about six inches above her bed. Then came the letters, in handwriting that wasn't hers, signed "Donald". It followed her to her job at Selfridges; she lost the job, then her friends. The disturbances ran twelve years. A reporter who slept in her room watched her pulled from the bed. In 2021, in her eighties, she told the BBC the same story her father told in 1956 — and never changed a word.
