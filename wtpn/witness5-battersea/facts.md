# WITNESS 5 — Battersea Poltergeist, 1956. Witness: Shirley Hitchings.

Every fact used in the episode, with sources. Human texture (interiority, staging) is
dramatised; no invented facts, no invented names.

## Sources
- Psi Encyclopedia (SPR): "Battersea Poltergeist (1956)" — psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk/articles/battersea-poltergeist-1956/
- History Hit: "The Terrifying Case of the Battersea Poltergeist"
- SPR book review: Shirley Hitchings & James Clark, *The Poltergeist Prince of London* (The History Press, 2013)
- BBC Radio 4 / Danny Robins, *The Battersea Poltergeist* (2021) — Shirley's first radio
  interview, given in her eighties ("crystal clear memories" — production interviews)

## Facts used
- 63 Wycliffe Road, Battersea, South London. Phenomena began late January 1956.
- Family: Wally Hitchings (father, train driver, 47), Kitty (mother, chronic arthritis),
  Shirley (15, seamstress at Selfridges), grandmother Ethel upstairs (73, retired nurse;
  died of a stroke during the disturbances).
- THE KEY: a small silver-coloured key appeared on Shirley's bed/pillow. Wally tried it
  in every lock in the house; it fitted none. Left in the kitchen overnight, it was found
  on Shirley's bed again next morning. (Psi Encyclopedia)
- KNOCKING: rapping/banging noises centred on wherever Shirley was; grew until audible
  from the street; neighbours complained; police and surveyors investigated and found no
  source. (Psi; History Hit: "deafening bangs... shaking the walls, floor and furniture")
- LEVITATION — Wally's account to the press, verbatim: "Shirley said the bedclothes were
  being pulled under her so we got hold of them and felt that they were being tugged by
  force. While this was going on we saw that Shirley was being lifted out of her bed.
  She was rigid and about six inches above the bed." (quoted in Psi Encyclopedia)
- Objects thrown: chairs, slippers, gloves; on 18 Feb 1956 a glove Shirley dropped "flew
  up and hit her father in the face". Clocks, pots and pans, flying bedsheets. (Psi; HH)
- COMMUNICATION: tapping code (counts for yes/no) + alphabet cards; then handwritten
  notes "apparently not in Shirley's handwriting", signed "Donald"; Donald later claimed
  to be Louis XVII, the lost King of France. (Psi)
- WORK: phenomena followed Shirley to her job (seamstress at Selfridges); she was forced
  to take two weeks off; her association with the case cost her the job and friends.
  (Psi; History Hit)
- INVESTIGATION: Harold Chibbett (Harry Price's protégé) investigated from early March
  1956, for years. (Psi; multiple)
- REPORTER: Joyce Lewis slept in Shirley's bed and witnessed her "being clawed and
  pulled out of the bed". (Psi)
- PRESS/PUBLIC: Daily Mail sensational coverage; case raised on prime-time BBC TV;
  discussed in the House of Commons. (History Hit)
- DURATION: disturbances faded by 1968 — about twelve years. Shirley married in 1961.
  (Psi; History Hit)
- 2021: Shirley, in her eighties, gave her first radio interview about the case for the
  BBC series; she has never recanted. (BBC R4 The Battersea Poltergeist, 2021)

## Dramatised (allowed texture, no new facts)
- Interiority: "He thought I was playing a trick. I thought he was." / memory of the
  ceiling / "like it was being careful with me" (characterisation of the set-down).
- Staging of documented practices: Mum working the alphabet cards; surveyors "tapping
  the pipes"; "they checked" the handwriting.
- The close (authority frame + refusal to recant) dramatises her documented lifelong
  consistency and the 2021 BBC interview.

## DO NOT USE
- The "James Dean spirit" 1957 subplot (tabloid-flavoured, weakens credibility).
- The seance photo (a known double-exposure fake — never imply it's real).
- Grandmother Ethel's death as a story beat (real bereavement, keep out).
- Precise letter counts (sources conflict: "up to 60/day" unverified; say "letters came,
  sometimes many in a day" at most).
