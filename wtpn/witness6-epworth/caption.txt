Epworth, Lincolnshire, 1716 — for two months something knocked through the rectory of the Wesley family: three, a stop, and three again, night after night. Their mother's letter survives: noises "as if several people were walking" overhead, money poured out at her feet, "all the bottles under the stairs dashed into a thousand pieces" — and nothing broken. The guard dog hid before the noises began. Their father challenged it aloud — "come to me in my study, that am a man" — and the next evening his study door was flung against him. It answered his made-up knock, pattern for pattern. One daughter was lifted up with the bed she sat on. Thirty years later the eldest daughter wrote it all again to her brother John — the John Wesley who founded the Methodist church — and called it "that wonderful thing called by us Jeffery".
