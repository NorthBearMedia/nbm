# WITNESS 6 — Epworth Rectory "Old Jeffery", 1716-17. Witness: Emilia (Emily) Wesley.

Interview framed at her documented 16 Feb 1750 letter to her brother John Wesley
(she was 24 during the events; ~58 at the letter). Every fact below is from the
family's contemporaneous letters/journal as reproduced in John Wesley's Arminian
Magazine account (1784), Priestley (1791), Clarke's Memoirs of the Wesley Family
(1823), and modern summaries citing them.

## Facts used
- Epworth Rectory, Lincolnshire; disturbances Dec 1716 – end of Jan 1717 (~2 months).
- Family: Rev. Samuel Wesley (rector), Susanna, and the children at home incl.
  Emily (~24, eldest at home), Hetty, Nancy; servants. John Wesley (13, away at
  school) later collected the accounts; he and Charles founded Methodism.
- FIRST SOUNDS (Susanna's letter): the maid heard "several dismal groans, like a
  person in extremes, at the point of death" at the dining-room door, 1 Dec 1716;
  family initially dismissed it; then knocking "three and stopped and then thrice
  again", night after night; each family member heard it in turn.
- SUSANNA's 12 Jan 1717 LETTER (verbatim): noise "as if several people were
  walking; then run up and down the stairs"; "as if somebody had emptied a bag of
  money at my feet"; "as if all the bottles under the stairs (which were many) had
  been dashed into a thousand pieces"; "like the winding up of a jack"; "like a
  carpenter planing deals". Nothing found displaced or broken.
- MASTIFF: a large dog acquired; it whimpered/hid when the phenomena came; crept
  into bed and howled as the door latch began to jar — reacted before the humans.
- SAMUEL SR's CONFRONTATION (documented challenge): "Thou deaf and dumb devil,
  why dost thou fright these children...? Come to me in my study, that am a man!"
  The following evening his study door was slammed/pushed hard against him. He
  knocked his own made-up pattern; the knocks REPEATED HIS PATTERN back. He kept
  a journal of the disturbances as they happened.
- PRAYERS FOR THE KING: violent knocking specifically at the prayers for King
  George — the "Jacobite ghost" reading. Documented across the family accounts.
- SIGHTINGS: badger-like creature seen (ran from under a bed / in the kitchen);
  something "like a man in a loose nightgown trailing after him" going down the
  stairs; Hetty trembled strongly in her sleep before/at the knockings.
- NANCY: "was once lifted up with the bed in which she sat."
- EMILY's 16 Feb 1750 letter to John (verbatim phrase): "that wonderful thing
  called by us Jeffery" — still affirming 33 years later; family never recanted.
- Emily born 1692 → 24 years old in Dec 1716. ✓

## Dramatised (texture only, no new facts)
- "We laughed at her" (initial dismissal is documented; the laugh is staging).
- "The dog knew before we did" (inference from documented latch/howl sequence).
- "Like a guest that had said what it came to say" (characterisation of the end).
- Emily narrating her mother's/father's/sisters' documented experiences as the
  family member she was; all attributions kept ("her words, in her letter").

## DO NOT USE
- Who coined the name "Jeffery" (sources conflict: Hetty vs Emily) — say
  "called by us Jeffery" (Emily's own phrase), never attribute the coining.
- The Wikipedia-flagged later embellishments (deafening horn, etc.) — skeptics
  note these grew in retelling; stick to the letter/journal material above.
- Any claim the ghost SPOKE. It never did.
