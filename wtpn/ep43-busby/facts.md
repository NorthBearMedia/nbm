# EP43 — The Busby Stoop Chair, Thirsk, North Yorkshire — verified facts

CONFIRMED (multiple independent sources — museum, press, standard tellings):
1. Thomas Busby — hanged 1702 at Sandhutton crossroads near Thirsk, North Yorkshire, for the
   murder of Daniel Auty (also spelled Awety/Autie), his father-in-law and coin-forging partner.
   Busby's body was displayed on a gibbet (the "stoop") at the crossroads.
2. The inn at that crossroads became known as the Busby Stoop Inn — it stood there until the
   2010s (later a restaurant). The chair known as "Busby's chair" / "the Dead Man's Chair"
   stood in that inn.
3. The curse legend: Busby is said to have cursed the chair — sudden death to anyone who sits
   in it. STANDARD TELLING — attribute as "the story says". Variants: he was drinking in his
   favourite chair when arrested / cursed it from the gallows. Do NOT pick one as fact.
4. WWII: RAF Skipton-on-Swale (RCAF squadrons) was nearby; aircrew drank at the inn. The
   telling: those who sat in the chair did not come back from operations. Attribute: "the
   airmen began to say". (Widely reported; not an official record.)
5. Post-war deaths tied to the chair by the landlord and local press (hitchhiker struck by a
   car; a builder who fell through a roof the same afternoon; road deaths). Attribute to "the
   landlord kept count" — do NOT state professions as independent fact beyond "a hitchhiker",
   "a builder" (both in standard press tellings).
6. 1978: landlord Tony Earnshaw gave the chair to Thirsk Museum on condition it never be sat
   in again. CONFIRMED — museum acknowledges the donation and condition.
7. The chair HANGS today from the wall of Thirsk Museum, suspended well above the floor,
   explicitly so that no one can ever sit in it. CONFIRMED — museum's own display/statements.
   This is the present-tense anchor (T1).
8. Furniture historian (Dr Adam Bowett, 2014) dated the chair's construction to c.1840 —
   i.e. it cannot be the chair Busby sat in. SOURCE CONFLICT → conservative wording: never
   claim it is literally Busby's 1702 chair; call it "the chair from the inn", "the chair
   that carries his name". The museum still will not let anyone sit in it — which is the story.

CUT / DO NOT USE:
- Any named individual victim (names circulate but are not reliably sourced).
- A specific death count ("63 deaths" etc. — tabloid numbers, unverifiable).
- Claiming the 1840 dating publicly in the episode (deflates; we simply avoid claiming the
  chair is original — wording stays honest either way).
- "Cursed by Busby on the gallows" as fact — it is the legend's frame, attributed.
