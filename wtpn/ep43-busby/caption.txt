Thirsk, North Yorkshire, England — the museum here keeps a chair bolted to the wall, five feet off the floor, so that nobody can ever sit in it again. They call it the Dead Man's Chair. It stood in an inn at the crossroads where Thomas Busby was hanged in 1702 and left to rot on a post — and the story says he cursed it as he died: sudden death to any man who sits in my chair. Wartime bomber crews said the ones who sat in it didn't come back. After the war the landlord watched men laugh, sit down, and be dead inside the week — until 1978, when he gave the chair to Thirsk Museum on one condition: nobody sits in it, ever again. It is still up on that wall today. People still ask.
