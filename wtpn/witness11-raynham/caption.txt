Raynham Hall, Norfolk, September 1936 — two Country Life photographers were shooting the main staircase of a Norfolk manor. The dullest job in photography. Then the assistant saw it: "a vapoury form gradually assuming the appearance of a woman", descending the stairs towards them — his own published words. He shouted; the lens cap came off; the flash fired. Captain Provand, head under the focusing cloth, saw nothing and swore there could be nothing on the plate but a staircase. They were still arguing in the darkroom when the negative came up — and she was on it. Harry Price, the era's great exposer of fake mediums, questioned both men and examined the negative: "I could not shake their story, and I had no right to disbelieve them... the negative is entirely innocent of any faking." The house already had a name for her — the Brown Lady, seen on those stairs for a century. The photograph became the most famous ghost picture in the world, and ninety years of experts still argue about it.
