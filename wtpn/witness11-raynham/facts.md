# WITNESS 11 — Raynham Hall / the Brown Lady photograph, 19 Sep 1936.
# Witness: Indre Shira, Country Life photographer.

The channel covered Raynham in the OLD cinematic era (live-titles: tiktok 1039v,
youtube) — well past the 3-week gap; this is the witness-format retell from the
photographers' own published account.

## Facts used (verbatim where quoted)
- 19 Sep 1936: Captain Hubert C. Provand (photographer) and Indre Shira
  (assistant/partner), on assignment for Country Life, photographing Raynham
  Hall, Norfolk. They had taken one photograph of the main staircase and were
  setting up a second.
- SHIRA (verbatim, published): saw "a vapoury form gradually assuming the
  appearance of a woman" moving down the stairs towards them.
- Mechanics (per Price's summary of their account): Shira saw the apparition
  descending "at the precise moment when Captain Provand's head was under the
  black cloth. A shout — and the cap was off and the flashbulb fired."
- Provand saw nothing (under the focusing cloth); disbelieved until the
  negative was developed and the figure appeared on the staircase.
- Published in Country Life, 26 Dec 1936, with the photograph.
- HARRY PRICE (verbatim, published): "I will say at once I was impressed...
  I could not shake their story, and I had no right to disbelieve them."
  "Only collusion between the two men would account for the ghost if it is a
  fake. The negative is entirely innocent of any faking." Price was known for
  exposing fraudulent mediums.
- The Brown Lady: the Hall's resident apparition, seen on the stairs/in the
  house for over a century before 1936 (Captain Marryat's 1836 encounter is
  documented in Florence Marryat's 1891 memoir). Identified in lore with Lady
  Dorothy Walpole.
- The photograph remains the most famous ghost photograph in the world;
  experts still argue about it (sceptical theories: double exposure, grease,
  light artefact — never conclusively established).
