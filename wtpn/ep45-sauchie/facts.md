# EP45 — The Sauchie Poltergeist, 1960 — verified facts

CONFIRMED (standard documented record; the case is one of Britain's best-attested
poltergeist cases, analysed by A.R.G. Owen in "Can We Explain the Poltergeist?" 1964):
1. Virginia Campbell, 11 years old, recently arrived from Donegal, Ireland, living with
   her brother's family in Park Crescent, Sauchie, Clackmannanshire, Scotland. The
   disturbances began in late November 1960.
2. Phenomena at the house: loud knocking that followed Virginia (heard by neighbours),
   a heavy sideboard seen to move out from the wall and back, objects moving, violent
   rippling of her pillow, the linen chest by her bed rocking/moving. WITNESSED BY:
   Dr W. H. Nisbet (her GP), Dr William Logan and (Dr) Sheila Logan, Rev T. W. Lund,
   the family, neighbours — named adults who gave accounts. A tape recording of the
   knocking was made (broadcast on the BBC at the time).
3. SCHOOL: her teacher, Miss Margaret Stewart, stated she saw the lid of an EMPTY desk
   near Virginia rise and fall on its own during class, and on another occasion saw
   Virginia's desk/table rise from the floor, and a vase/bowl move across her own desk
   when Virginia approached it. Other pupils were present. This is the case's most
   famous beat — REPORT AS WHAT THE TEACHER REPORTED.
4. Ministers held a service of intercession at the house (late 1960). The phenomena
   faded through early 1961.
5. The case was reported in the national press at the time ("the Sauchie poltergeist")
   and remains formally unexplained. Owen concluded the phenomena were genuinely
   anomalous ("no explanation in terms of trickery or physical causes").
6. It followed the girl, not the house — manifestations occurred wherever Virginia was
   (house, school, briefly when she stayed elsewhere — soft wording: "it went where
   she went").
