South Shields, Tyneside, 2006 — two experienced investigators moved into an ordinary family house expecting pipes, drafts, or a hoax. They stayed for months and compiled five hundred photographs and hours of tape. What they logged, watching the family's hands the entire time: a toy flung with force across a bedroom, a rubber ball that appeared from nowhere — hot to the touch — a kitchen knife lying in the middle of a child's bedroom floor, a carving knife that hurled itself across the kitchen in front of them, scratches opening across the father's back, words appearing on a child's doodle board — "go now", "stop it now" — and threatening text messages sent from phones that were switched off. The investigator who documented Enfield in 1977 wrote the book's foreword. The family's names were changed in print; the investigators published everything else and never withdrew a word.
