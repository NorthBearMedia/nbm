# WITNESS 12 — The Cheltenham Ghost, 1882-89. Witness: Rosina Clara Despard.

Rosina Despard wrote up her own haunting as a scientific record; the SPR published
it in Proceedings (1892) as "Record of a Haunted House" under the pseudonym
"R.C. Morton" / "Rose Morton". She was a medical student during the events and
qualified as a physician while they were still going on.

The channel ran a CINEMATIC Cheltenham episode on 17 Aug (EP39). Witness retell is
21 days later (7 Sep) = the 3-week gap is met exactly.

## Facts used (verbatim where quoted)
- House: Garden Reach (later Donore, later St Anne's), Pittville Circus Road,
  Cheltenham. Captain Despard moved in April 1882 with an invalid wife and six
  children. Rosina was in her late teens (19), studying medicine.
- FIRST SIGHTING June 1882: heard someone at her door; opened it, saw no one;
  went a few steps along the passage and saw "the figure of a tall lady, dressed
  in black, standing at the head of the stairs". She followed; her candle-end
  burnt itself out.
- DESCRIPTION (verbatim): "a tall lady, dressed in black of a soft woollen
  material... The face was hidden in a handkerchief held in the right hand."
  Widow's weeds, widow's cuffs on both wrists; something like a bonnet with a
  long veil or hood. The sound of the soft woollen material as it moved.
- FREQUENCY: ~6 sightings in the first two years; peak July-August 1884;
  declining by 1887; ceased by 1889.
- 17 PEOPLE in total saw the figure between 1882 and 1886 — many took it for a
  living woman at first. Others heard the footsteps.
- 29 JANUARY 1884: she SPOKE to it; got "a slight gasp" and no words.
- EXPERIMENTS: she strung threads across the stairs; the figure passed and the
  threads were NOT displaced. She could not touch it; when she tried to corner
  it in a room, "it vanished".
- SHE WROTE IT DOWN AS IT HAPPENED, and sent the record to the SPR.
- FREDERIC MYERS (verbatim): called it one of the "most remarkable and best
  authenticated instances of 'haunting' on record", and described her as
  "a lady of scientific training, now preparing to be a physician".
- Tentatively identified from a photograph as Imogen Swinhoe, second wife of a
  former owner (Henry Swinhoe); Imogen died 1878. NOTE: identification is
  tentative — script says "they thought", never states it as fact.
- ROSINA'S CAREER: became the 23rd woman to graduate with a medical degree in
  England, and physician at Holloway women's prison.
