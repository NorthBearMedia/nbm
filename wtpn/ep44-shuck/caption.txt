Blythburgh, Suffolk, England — the claw-marks are still burned into the church door, and they still show them to visitors. They call them the devil's fingerprints. In August 1577, during a Sunday storm, a pamphlet printed that same year says a black dog the size of a calf came up the aisle and killed a man and a boy where they knelt — then struck a second church twelve miles away. Two more dead at prayer. East Anglia gave it a name: Black Shuck. They say seeing him means a death within the year — yours, or someone's you love. People never stopped reporting him on the marshes and in the churchyards. And in 2014, archaeologists at Leiston Abbey dug up the skeleton of a dog seven feet long. The papers only had one name for it. 🐾 Would you put your hand on the marks?
