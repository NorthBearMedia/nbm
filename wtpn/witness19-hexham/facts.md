# WITNESS 19 — The Hexham Heads, 1971. Witness: Dr Anne Ross, Celtic scholar.

Anne Ross (1925–2012) was a Celtic scholar with an MA and PhD from Edinburgh, a
Research Fellow in the School of Scottish Studies and later in Archaeology at
Southampton, and author of "Pagan Celtic Britain" — the standard text on the
Celtic cult of the head. She described her experience on camera on BBC
Nationwide, broadcast Friday 20 February 1976 (reporter Luke Casey); Fortean
Times issue 15 (April 1976) printed a near-verbatim transcript. She had already
gone public in the press (The Journal, 14 January 1974). This episode
dramatises HER account.

## Facts used (verbatim where quoted)
- 1971: two boys, brothers, dug up two small carved stone heads in the garden
  at 3 Rede Avenue, Hexham, Northumberland. (Boys NOT named — they were
  children and are likely living.)
- In that house, before the heads left it: a boy's hair was pulled in the night
  and his mother saw a figure leave the house. (Wikipedia + Burials & Beyond
  agree on boy/hair/mother.)
- The heads went to Anne Ross for study. Her own published position on Celtic
  heads: venerated "as a symbol of divinity and the powers of the otherworld…
  the very seat of the soul".
- ROSS, VERBATIM (Nationwide, 20 Feb 1976): "It was about six feet high,
  slightly stooping, and it was black, you know, against the white door, and it
  was half-animal and half-man. The upper part I would have said was a wolf,
  and the lower part was human, and I would've again said that it was covered
  with a kind of black, very dark fur, and it went out — I just saw it clearly
  and then it disappeared."
- She woke around 2am, cold and frightened, and then FOLLOWED it: out of the
  bedroom, down the stairs, where it went over the banister in one movement and
  towards the back of the house. She noted this was unlike her.
- Her daughter Berenice, coming home from school, saw a large dark figure on
  the stairs that went over the banister and into a corridor and vanished.
  (Referred to only as "my daughter" — she may be living.)
- The whole household, and the cat, saw or heard it. Ross also reported a cold
  presence and her study door bursting open.
- ROSS to the press (The Journal, 14 Jan 1974): "half-human, half beast, like a
  werewolf".
- ROSS, VERBATIM (closing her Nationwide account): "The day the heads were
  removed from the house, everybody including my husband said, you know, it's
  as if a cloud had lifted."
- COUNTER-EVIDENCE (must be included):
  * Desmond Craigie, Evening Chronicle, VERBATIM: "I made them – about 16 years
    ago. I made the heads from bits of stone and mortar simply to amuse my
    daughter when she was a little girl. I actually made three but one appears
    to have got lost. They were out in the garden for years. I definitely made
    them." He said 1956, from local stone, sand and water; he had lived in the
    house the Robsons later occupied; he worked for a concrete company.
  * Professor Dearman, University of Newcastle, concluded the heads "had been
    moulded artificially rather than carved" — consistent with Craigie.
- The heads later vanished; their whereabouts are still unknown.
- Ross never withdrew her account; she spoke about it in 1974 and again on
  television in 1976. No retraction is on record.

## Dramatised (texture only)
- The heads sitting in her study as "a job of work"; "I took notes"; standing on
  her own staircase; "I am not a brave woman and I had no business following
  it"; the cost line about her reputation; interviewer question cards; ordering.

## DO NOT USE
- 1972 as the discovery year (Wikipedia flags it as an error).
- The species of the creature seen at the Hexham house — sources say goat, sheep
  and "half-man half-beast". Say only that the mother saw a figure leave. Ross's
  own creature is a wolf because SHE said wolf, on camera.
- The mother's first name — sources split between Nelly and Ellen. Do not name.
- The Robson boys' names, or the daughter's name.
- Any claim the heads were proven ancient or Celtic. Dearman's finding says
  moulded. State the counter-evidence plainly.
- Don Robins' radiation / "stone tape" theorising, and the presenter's
  stone-tape question, as fact — it is speculation. Omit.
- Calling the thing a werewolf as fact. She said "like a werewolf"; the press
  called it one.
- Any suggestion Ross recanted, or that she profited.
