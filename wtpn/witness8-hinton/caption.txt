Hinton Ampner, Hampshire, 1771 — the lady of a Georgian manor wrote down, at the time, exactly what her household endured for six years: a gentleman in drab clothes seen walking into an empty room, a woman in rustling silk who swept past the cook and vanished, doors slapped shut "with vehemence" and found locked and bolted, explosions of sound "as loud as the bursting of a cannon", three voices in discourse in empty rooms — and a man's plodding footsteps walking to the foot of her bed. Her brother — the future Admiral Earl St Vincent, one of the Royal Navy's greatest commanders — sat up armed through the nights, heard it himself, and told her no natural cause appeared and no house was fit to live in where such noises were heard. She left. The house never kept another tenant and was pulled down in 1793 — when workmen found a small skull under one of the floors. It was never examined.
