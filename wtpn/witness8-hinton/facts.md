# WITNESS 8 — Hinton Ampner, Hampshire, 1765–1771. Witness: Mary Ricketts.

Mary Ricketts wrote "Narrative of the Strange Events at Hinton Ampner" in 1772
for her children; the manuscript is preserved and her reputation for
truthfulness was never impeached. Her brother Captain John Jervis became
Admiral of the Fleet, Earl St Vincent — one of the Royal Navy's greatest
commanders. This episode dramatises HER narrative.

## Sources
- Strange Company blog "The Haunting of Hinton Ampner" (extensive verbatim
  quotes from the Ricketts narrative — all quotes below from there)
- Gordon Bonnet "The haunting of Hinton Ampner" / Skeptophilia / Haunted Wiki
  (case overview, 1772 manuscript, Jervis investigation, 1793 demolition)

## Facts used (verbatim where quoted)
- SERVANT SIGHTINGS 1765–67: nurse Elizabeth Brelsford saw "a gentleman in a
  drab-coloured suit of clothes go into the yellow room" — room empty on
  search; groom George Turner saw "a man in a drab-coloured coat" in the great
  hall at night, all male servants in bed; kitchen servants heard a woman come
  downstairs, clothes rustling "as of the stiffest silk"; a female figure
  rushed past and vanished (cook Dame Brown witness).
- MARY, from 1765: noises "as of people shutting, or rather slapping doors
  with vehemence"; husband searched repeatedly, servants in their apartments,
  "no appearance of disorder".
- 2 APRIL 1771 (verbatim): "I heard one or more persons walking to and fro in
  the lobby adjoining. I got out of bed and listened at the door for the space
  of twenty minutes… I distinctly heard the walking with the addition of a
  loud noise like pushing strongly against a door." Search with Elizabeth
  Godin: window shut, chimney sealed, door locked — nobody.
- SOUNDS (verbatim fragments): "knocking, opening and shutting of doors,
  talking, explosions, sometimes as loud as the bursting of cannon"; "a shrill
  female voice would begin, and then two others with deeper and manlike tone
  seemed to join in the discourse"; "a hollow murmuring that seemed to possess
  the whole house".
- SUMMER 1770, YELLOW BEDCHAMBER (verbatim): "I plainly heard the footsteps of
  a man, with plodding step, walking towards the foot of my bed." She fled to
  the nursery; search found nothing.
- 7 MAY 1771: "the great hall door directly under me was slapped to with the
  utmost violence, so as to shake my room perceivably" — found "fast locked
  and bolted as usual".
- THE CAT: would "suddenly slink down as if struck with the greatest terror,
  conceal herself under my chair", later emerge unconcerned.
- JERVIS/LUTTRELL VIGIL, Aug 1771: Captain Luttrell heard "the footsteps of a
  person walking across the lobby"; "something flitted past him" at the door;
  staircase door found fast secured; her brother heard "dreadful groans";
  their joint verdict: "no house was fit to live in where such noises were
  heard, and no natural cause appeared". He pressed her to leave; she left
  (late July/Aug 1771), health broken by sleeplessness.
- ATTESTATION (verbatim): "To the Almighty and Unerring Judgment of Heaven and
  Earth I dare appeal for the truth, to the best of my memory and
  comprehension, of what I here relate."
- AFTERMATH: the house could not keep tenants and was demolished in 1793;
  workmen found under a floor a small skull ("said to be that of a monkey" in
  one account; other retellings claim a child's bones) in/with a small box —
  "never brought forward by any regular inquiry, or professional opinion".
- John Jervis later Admiral Earl St Vincent ✓ (public record).

## Dramatised (texture only)
- "You do. Servants' tales." initial-dismissal staging; the cat beat phrased
  in her register; interviewer question cards; ordering of beats.

## DO NOT USE
- WHOSE skull it was — sources conflict (monkey vs child). Say a small skull
  was found and never examined. Never say "a child's skull" as fact.
- The Stawell scandal/murder theories as fact — omit entirely (speculation).
- Any claim the ghost was identified.
