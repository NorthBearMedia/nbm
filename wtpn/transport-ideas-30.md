# Transport story — 30 ideas (27 Nov)

The user wants to go back to the transport idea and get it right. Lessons from Handover and Doors: one simple
scary thing you can hear or be told in a line; no plot machinery (door alerts, trailer swaps); likeable people.
Nothing with people shut inside a trailer (too close to the 2019 Essex lorry deaths).

1. Come In — A wagon nobody's expecting pulls up at the yard gate at 3am, and the driver buzzes the planner to be let in, friendly as anything, though the barrier's already up. Scary bit: he won't come through until someone says the words "come in", and the new lad on the yard radio just has.
2. Reversing — Every wagon's parked up and the yard's dead when a reversing alarm starts somewhere out in the dark. Scary bit: it's one of the talking ones, "Caution, this vehicle is reversing", and it gets closer and closer to the office window with nothing out there moving.
3. Rest Room — The internal phone rings from the drivers' rest room at 3am, and the planner knows it's empty because he locked it himself. Scary bit: when he answers, he can hear his own office down the line (his radio, his kettle) and someone breathing right next to him.
4. Steamed Up — On a freezing night the security guard radios the planner that every locked, empty cab in the yard has steamed up from the inside, like someone's breathing in all of them. Scary bit: as he walks the row with his torch, one cab window gets wiped clear from the inside, then the next, then the next, coming down the row towards him.
5. The Pallet — Nobody can find paperwork for a black-wrapped pallet sat in the middle of the yard, and it's warm to the touch. Scary bit: the forklift driver radios in that the wrap is going in and out like it's breathing, and by morning it's gone out on someone's trailer, and that driver's stopped answering.
6. How's My Driving? — At 3am the planner's phone starts ringing with strangers calling the number on the back of wagon 41 on the M62. Scary bit: every caller sees something on the roof of the trailer, each one a bit closer to the cab, and the driver can't see a thing.
7. Home Without Him — A driver rings the planner from a lay-by sixty miles away to say he's broken down, just as his wagon reverses itself into its bay outside the office window, empty. Scary bit: the planner climbs up into the empty cab to look, and on the phone the driver whispers, "Mick, someone's just got in my cab."
8. Clicks — The yard shunter keys his radio at 3am but won't speak, so the planner says "one click for yes, two for no" and starts asking. Scary bit: Are you hiding? One click. Is it close? One click. Can it hear us? One click. "Is that you clicking?" Two clicks.
9. Bunk — A driver's lying in his bunk behind the curtain in a lay-by on the A66, on the phone to the planner, when he hears the air seat sigh as someone sits in the driver's seat. Scary bit: the engine starts, the handbrake hisses off, and the wagon pulls away with him still behind the curtain.
10. Seatbelt — On a night run the passenger seatbelt warning starts chiming, because the seat sensor says someone's sat there. Scary bit: he's alone, and the chiming stops with a click, like someone's just put their belt on.
11. Voice Note — The planner gets a four-minute voice note from a driver at 3am and plays it on speaker. Scary bit: it's just him singing along to the radio in his cab, until the passenger door opens, the singing stops, and the recording keeps going.
12. Dedications — A night trunker's radio drifts onto a little local station he's never heard of, and the DJ starts dedicating songs to him by name, mentioning the bend he's on. Scary bit: the next one goes out to his wife at home, "who's just heard the back door go."
13. Blind Spot — On an empty moor road, the sensor that warns a driver about cyclists alongside his wagon starts beeping and won't stop. Scary bit: he puts his foot down to 56 and it stays with him, then the sensor on the other side starts too.
14. Out of Hours — A driver runs out of legal driving hours in a lay-by on the moor, so he has to stay put for eleven hours or lose his licence, whatever's outside. Scary bit: with the engine off and the handbrake on, the gravel starts crunching under his tyres; something is pushing 44 tonnes of wagon, slowly, at walking pace.
15. The Heater — A driver sleeping in his cab in the snow rings the planner, sure something's circling the wagon, and the planner thinks the night heater's fumes are getting to him and begs him to open a window. Scary bit: he won't ("That's what it wants, Mick"), and you never find out which of them is right.
16. Recovery — A driver broken down in a lay-by on the A9 is told recovery's on its way, and twenty minutes later a truck pulls up and the man knows his name. Scary bit: the planner hears him climb up into the recovery truck's cab, and then the real recovery firm rings: they're still an hour away.
17. Memorial — A driver parks for the night beside a roadside memorial, flowers tied to the barrier for a crash thirty years ago. Scary bit: at 3am he hears cellophane rustling as someone changes the flowers right next to his cab, and the new card has his name on it, and tomorrow's date.
18. Dead Man's Run — The planner finally gets hold of a driver who's missed his check-in, and he's cheerful, just says the road's gone dark and he's a bit lost. Scary bit: the police are on the other line saying he died in a crash two hours ago, and the driver says, "There's a fella here says he'll show me the way."
19. Scotch Corner — A driver heading north on the A1 passes Scotch Corner services, then passes it again, and again, though he's never turned off. Scary bit: the planner checks the tracker: he's been parked at Scotch Corner with the engine off for an hour, and he can still see the road going past.
20. The Bell — A driver's hauling an old church bell to the foundry to be melted down, and parked up at 3am it starts to toll on its own. Scary bit: it's the old death knell (nine strokes for a man, then one for every year of his life), and the planner counts along out loud, and it stops on the driver's age.
21. Coffins — A driver's delivering a load of brand-new, empty coffins to undertakers, and one of them weighs as much as a man. Scary bit: parked up at 3am, he hears screws turning in the back, slowly, one at a time.
22. Phone Boxes — A driver's moving a load of old red phone boxes to a reclamation yard, and in a lay-by at 3am one of them starts to ring. Scary bit: none of them are wired to anything, and when it stops the next one rings, then the next, working down the trailer towards the doors.
23. Pigs — A driver's taking a full load of pigs to the abattoir at night, and two hundred of them go silent at once. Scary bit: he can hear them all crushing up against the front of the trailer behind his cab, as far as they can get from something at the back.
24. Heavy — A driver taking an empty trailer back over Shap at night says it's pulling like it's fully loaded, and getting heavier every mile. Scary bit: at the summit it goes light all at once, like something's got off, and the next driver up the pass rings in: there's someone walking down the middle of the road.
25. Footprints — A gritter driver on his night run over the Snake Pass radios the depot: there are bare footprints in the fresh snow ahead of him, going his way. Scary bit: mile after mile they stay fresh at thirty miles an hour, so whatever's making them is always just past his headlights, until they stop dead in the middle of the road.
26. The Walk — The M62 is shut by snow at Windy Hill and nothing's moved for hours, when a driver tells the planner the other drivers are getting out and walking up onto the moor. Scary bit: then he says, calm as anything, he thinks he'll go and have a look too, and the planner hears his cab door open.
27. Fog — Crawling over the Woodhead Pass in thick fog, a driver follows the tail lights of the wagon in front to stay on the road. Scary bit: the fog thins for a second and there's no wagon, just two red lights at trailer height, and they're turning round.
28. The Drowned Village — A night delivery's satnav takes a driver down to Haweswater, where the village of Mardale was drowned for a reservoir in the 1930s, and the customer rings to say she can see his headlights. Scary bit: his headlights are pointing at the water, and she says, "Keep coming, love, you're nearly here."
29. The Hitchhiker — Based on an old lorry drivers' legend from the A38 near Wellington in Somerset: a driver gives a lift to a man in a grey coat, drops him a few miles on, then sees him at the roadside ahead again, waving a torch. Scary bit: it keeps happening, each time a little further on, and the last time he's standing in the middle of the road.
30. Last Ferry — Racing for the night ferry at Cairnryan along the A75's Kinmount Straight, called Scotland's most haunted road, a driver keeps braking for figures stepping into his headlights that are gone when he stops. Scary bit: the last one doesn't vanish, and in his mirror he watches it walk round the back of the trailer to his passenger side.

My picks: 8 (Clicks), 9 (Bunk), 20 (The Bell).
