DOCUMENTED (sources: her written statement to Maurice Grosse dated 10 Sept 1977; her
Feb 1978 statement to BBC Scotland; SPR Psi Encyclopedia; case literature):
1. WPC Carolyn Heeps and a male colleague attended 284 Green Street, Enfield, North
   London, around 1 a.m. (call late August 1977). Mother (Peggy Hodgson) and four
   children present, frightened.
2. HER VERBATIM (statement): "I looked at the chair and noticed that it was wobbling
   slightly from side to side, I then saw the chair slide across the floor towards
   the kitchen wall." It moved approximately three to four feet, then came to rest.
   Nobody was near it / no physical contact.
3. She examined the chair — underneath, for wires or any mechanism — and found
   nothing. Her report: she "could not determine the cause of the movement."
4. She signed the statement within two weeks (10 Sept 1977) and repeated the account
   on camera (BBC, 1978). She never withdrew it.
5. Police concluded the matter was not a police matter; no further action.
6. Case context: phenomena at the house ran ~18 months; more than 30 witnesses
   reported furniture movement, thrown objects, knocking, the voice. Investigators:
   Maurice Grosse and Guy Lyon Playfair (SPR). The case remains disputed — skeptics
   (Gregory, Christopher, Nickell) attribute much to the children; the police
   officer's chair statement is the piece neither side ever shook.
