# WITNESS 15 — The Bromley Poltergeist, Grove Park allotments, 1973-75.
# Witness: Tony Elms, one of three pensioners running the Kentish Garden Guild.

NEVER COVERED by the channel in any format (checked ledger + live-titles).
Investigated on site by two Society for Psychical Research members who saw the
phenomena themselves; written up by one of them as a published monograph.

## Sources
- Manfred Cassirer, "The Persecution of Mr Tony Elms (The Bromley Poltergeist)",
  1993 — the case monograph (SPR investigator, wrote it up)
- Psi Encyclopedia (SPR), "Manfred Cassirer": in 1973 he "investigated a serious
  outbreak of poltergeist-type phenomena that disrupted a garden supplies depot
  in Bromley, London, run by Tony Elms"
- Strange Company, "The Poltergeist in the Allotment Shed" (detailed account,
  quotes below)

## Facts used
- The Kentish Garden Guild: three pensioners — Alfred Taylor, Tony Elms and
  Clifford Jewiss — running two sheds at the Grove Park allotments near Bromley,
  selling gardening supplies to other allotment holders in retirement.
- BEGAN 26 April 1973: strange powder hit the ceiling; a small jug flew across
  the room. Jewiss shut the jug in a covered box; it was found back on the floor.
- ESCALATION: objects circling the hut "like skittles"; fertiliser shooting out
  of bins; a SEVEN-POUND WEIGHT circling Taylor's head; bottles unscrewing
  themselves; half-hundredweight/half-ton fertiliser bags moving on their own;
  coins flying. A customer: "There were some right queer goings on."
- Elms attempted an exorcism after consulting others; those outside heard walls
  thudding and heavy iron doors swinging; he came out bruised, with a cut head.
  Next morning the shed looked "as if it had been hit by a bomb", with the sign
  of the cross "painted or scratched everywhere — on walls, on chairs, on bins".
- SPR INVESTIGATION (from Sept 1973): Pauline Runnells and Manfred Cassirer made
  several visits and concluded it was no hoax. THEY PERSONALLY WITNESSED: items
  suspended in the air; security bolts vanishing; violent blows on the walls;
  the shirt being RIPPED OFF Elms and a SAW pushed down his back; a flower bulb
  forced into his mouth; money vanishing.
- Automatic writing appeared, including the number 1659, question marks, letters
  and a friend's name; an impression like a child's face appeared on a shelf.
- Lasted nearly TWO YEARS — unusually long for a poltergeist — and stopped
  around the time construction of nearby garages finished (no causal link
  established; do NOT claim one).
- Never debunked: "Everyone who visited... agreed that something very strange
  went on, something that was not capable of being created by any human
  trickery."

## Dramatised (texture only)
- Elms' register as an ordinary retired man ("We sold seed potatoes"); the
  interiority of being singled out ("it had picked me"); interviewer cards.

## DO NOT USE
- Occult/demonic explanation as fact (the exorcism attempt is documented; the
  cause is not).
- Any claim the 1659 writing identifies a historical person or event.
- Claiming the garage construction caused or ended it.
- Any claim the three men profited or hoaxed it (investigators found no
  deception; leave the verdict with the reader).
