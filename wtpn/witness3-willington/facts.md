# WITNESS 3 — Dr Edward Drury, Willington Mill, 3 July 1840

WITNESS: Edward Drury, young surgeon of Sunderland. Wrote to mill-owner Joseph Procter
(Quaker) asking leave to sit up a night and "detect the imposture". Attended 3 July 1840
with Thomas Hudson, chemist. His letter of account is dated 13 July 1840 and was
published in the Victorian literature (Howitt; Richardson's Table Book; Henderson's
Folk-Lore of the Northern Counties — Wikisource sourced).

VERBATIM (Drury's letter): carried "a brace of pistols", planning to drop one "as if by
accident, before the miller, for fear he should presume to play tricks upon me" /
~11:50pm: "a noise, as if a number of people were pattering with their bare feet upon
the floor", then "a noise as if some one was knocking with his knuckles among our feet",
then "a hollow cough from the very room from which the apparition proceeded" / 12:50am,
eyes moving from his watch: the closet door "I distinctly saw open", "the figure of a
female, attired in greyish garments, with the head inclined downwards, and one hand
pressed upon the chest as if in pain", the right hand "extended towards the floor, with
the index finger pointing downwards" / it "advanced with an apparently cautious step
across the floor" toward the slumbering Hudson, right hand extended toward him / "I
rushed at it, giving at the time, as Mr. Procter states, a most awful yell, but instead
of grasping it I fell upon my friend" / ~3 hours of which he could give no clear account;
"carried downstairs in an agony of fear and terror" / closing certification: "I hereby
certify that the above account is strictly true and correct in every respect."
CONTEXT: Procter's own 1835 letter records earlier "credible witnesses to the apparition
of a woman in her grave-clothes at four separate times outside the house." The Procter
family remained some years more and later left; Drury's letter has been in print since
the Victorian period. Drury did not return.
