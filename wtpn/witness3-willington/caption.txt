Willington Mill, Tyneside, 1840 — a young Sunderland surgeon wrote to the mill's owner asking permission to sit up one night and expose the "haunting" as a hoax. He brought a friend, and a pair of loaded pistols for whoever was playing the tricks. At ten minutes to one, watching the landing by candlelight, he saw the closet door open — and a grey figure came out of it, head bent, one hand pressed to her chest, and reached for his sleeping friend. He rushed it, screaming, passed straight through where she stood, and was carried out of the house "in an agony of fear and terror" — his own words, in the letter he signed ten days later and certified "strictly true and correct in every respect." It has been in print for nearly two centuries. His account, dramatised from the published letters. 🎙️ Would you have signed it?
