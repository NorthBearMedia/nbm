Cardiff, Wales, 1989 — a repair-shop owner wrote to the Society for Psychical Research about the stones flying across his workshop, and the professor they sent watched one thrown at him within his first minute through the door. For two years Professor David Fontana investigated the thing the family came to call Pete: the ice-cold corner, the banknotes folded into the ceiling tiles, and the experiment nobody forgets — throw a stone into the corner, and the corner throws it back. Not bounced. Thrown. He published it as "A Responsive Poltergeist" and concluded it showed intent. His account, and the owner's, dramatised from their broadcast interviews and the SPR case reports. 🎙️ Would you have thrown the stone back?
