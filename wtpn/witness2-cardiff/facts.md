# WITNESS 2 — John Matthews, the Cardiff poltergeist ("Pete"), 1989-92

WITNESS: John Matthews, who ran a lawnmower/grass-machinery repair business in Cardiff,
Wales, with his brother and wife Pat. Investigated June 1989 - 1992 by Prof. David
Fontana (psychologist, Cardiff; later SPR president), published in the Journal of the
SPR as "A Responsive Poltergeist: A Case from South Wales" (1991, + 1992 follow-up).
Broadcast: ITV "Strange But True?" interviews (transcript sourced).

VERBATIM (broadcast, John Matthews): "We started in this building about fifteen years
ago, repairing all sorts of grass-cutting equipment." / "We were baffled that there
weren't any kids around here and nobody that could throw stones." / "I started to get
worried that there was something strange here in this building." / "There was no chance
of anybody being in that shop except us three." / "We were never really afraid of it.
It became part of the family."
VERBATIM (Pat Matthews): "in this one corner of the shop it got ice, ice cold" /
banknotes ("a fiver or a ten pound note") found "stuck in the ceiling tile" in the
mornings.
VERBATIM (Fontana): "As I entered the workshop, at that very moment a stone was thrown
across the room and bounced off a piece of machinery." / "You don't see the things in
midair, you hear them hit the wall and clatter to the ground, you look round and the
stone is there." John's reply that day: "see, it's Pete and he's saying hello."
DOCUMENTED: Fontana's retrieval experiments — stones thrown into the "active corner"
were returned/thrown back; his published conclusion: the phenomena showed "intent and
the rudimentary intelligence necessary to accomplish this intent"; he found no evidence
of trickery. Case faded ~1992; the family named it "Pete".
