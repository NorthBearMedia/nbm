Pontefract, West Yorkshire, 1968 — in an ordinary council house at 30 East Drive, a fine white dust fell out of mid-air, pools of water rose from a floor with no pipe beneath it, and a grandmother clock went down the stairs on its own. The press called it Mr Nobody; the family called it Fred. It centred on the fourteen-year-old daughter: thrown from her bed, pinned under furniture, woken beneath her own mattress — and one night dragged up the stairs by her throat, leaving finger marks her whole family saw. A tall hooded figure in black was seen in the house. In 1969 her mother hung garlic by the doors, and it stopped. The family told the same story to investigator Colin Wilson, who wrote it into a book still in print — and never took a word back. The house still stands; people pay to spend the night in her old room. Dramatised from the Pritchard family's documented accounts. 🎙️ Would you sleep at 30 East Drive?
