# TRANSMISSION 02 — "CORNER"  (Part 2)  — DRAFT v1, 24 Nov
Runtime ~1:50. Three voices. Mid-Wales hill farm, lambing, 2 a.m., rain.
From pitch #6 (23 Nov): something's in with the ewes; every sheep faces the same corner; he describes
it for four words and stops; the cut is him saying her name, twice, calmly.

Voices: OWEN (40s, hill farmer, in the lambing shed 300 m down the track) · NIA (his wife, in the
farmhouse kitchen with a weak lamb by the Aga; OUR radio is hers, on the kitchen table, so she is the
room voice: red light, dry) · EMLYN (60s/70s, neighbour across the valley, also up lambing).
No mobile signal in the valley; the handhelds stay on all lambing and the two farms share a channel.

0:00  Rain on a tin roof. A shed full of sheep, the low constant noise of it. Squelch.
OWEN:  —uple more in the big pen, then that's me done. The one with the bad foot's had twins. Both up and feeding.
NIA:   Ah, good girl. This one's taking the bottle now. Bit of fight in her.        (the lamb by the Aga bleats, in the room)
EMLYN: That you two still up? I've had three since midnight. Every one of them coming out backwards.
OWEN:  Go to bed, Emlyn.
EMLYN: Go to bed yourself.

0:22  Gates rattle. The ewes scramble.
OWEN:  Hang on. Something's set them off. They're all piled up at the gate end.
OWEN:  Emlyn, if that's your bloody dog again, I swear.
EMLYN: Both my dogs are in. They're lying here by the fire.
OWEN:  Well, something's in here with them.
NIA:   Fox?
OWEN:  Probably. I'll have a look.

0:40  The sheep stop. Not fade: stop. Rain on the tin roof, nothing else.
OWEN:  Nia. They've gone quiet.
NIA:   What do you mean, quiet?
OWEN:  Sixty ewes and not a sound. They're all stood facing the back corner. Every one of them. Same corner.
EMLYN: Owen. Come out of there.
OWEN:  It's a fox, Emlyn. I've got the torch.

0:58  Straw underfoot. His breathing. He keeps the button down.
OWEN:  Right. I'm at the back pen. There's—
       (three seconds of rain)
OWEN:  It's not a fox.
       (he doesn't let go of the button: rain on the roof, nothing else, four seconds; then the transmission drops)
NIA:   Owen? ... Owen, what is it?
EMLYN: Nia. His shed lights have gone. I can see your place from here. The whole shed's gone dark.
NIA:   Owen, answer me.

1:25  Squelch. Owen. Rain on the tin roof behind him. Calm. Very calm.
OWEN:  Nia.
NIA:   I'm here. I'm here, love. What's in the corner?
       (nothing)
EMLYN: Nia, there's someone crossing your yard. Under the light. Coming up to the house.
EMLYN: Owen, is that you?
       (nothing on the radio; in the kitchen, the lamb, which has bleated softly under every one of Nia's lines, stops)

1:45  Three slow knocks on the back door. In the room with us.
NIA:   (whispering, not into the radio) Owen?
       (from the other side of the door; not on the radio; the light stays dark)
OWEN:  Nia.
Cut to black.

## Production notes
- Sheep bed under every shed transmission; it stops dead at 0:40. The silence is the scare.
- Owen's calm "Nia." on the radio has the tin roof behind it: he is still in the shed. The second
  "Nia." is dry and muffled through a door, no radio, light off. Watch the light pays off again.
- Kitchen: Aga hum, the lamb's soft bleats under Nia; they stop before the knock.
- Crest lessons applied: everyone on the channel hears everything, so nobody shouts for someone with a
  radio; no farming jargon; no detail contradicts a later one; "It's not a fox" is him stopping, not a
  cut, so it is generated as a complete line; one take per character with a long lead-in; accent-gated
  (CommonAccent has a Wales class).
- Voices to source: three Welsh (none proven yet; the bible lists Wales as a gap).
- Visual: farmhouse kitchen table at night, radio, Aga glow, lamb in a box under a towel, back door
  with a rain-streaked window; reuse the rain and light compositing from Crest.
