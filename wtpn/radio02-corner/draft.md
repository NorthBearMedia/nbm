Runtime ~1:45. Three voices. Hill farm, Co. Mayo, lambing, 2 a.m., rain.

Voices: CONOR (40s, farmer; starts in the hay shed by the house, the lambing shed is 300 m down the track) · MAEVE (his wife, in the
farmhouse kitchen with a weak lamb by the range; OUR radio is hers, on the kitchen table: room voice,
red light) · GERRY (60s, neighbour across the valley, also up lambing).
No phone signal in the valley; both farms keep the handhelds on the same channel through lambing.

0:00  Rain and wind. An open-sided hay shed: a ewe complaining close by. Squelch.
CONOR: —ill in the hay shed with the lame one. Her foot's in a terrible state. I'll do the lambing shed after this.
MAEVE: Grand. This one's taking the bottle now. Bit of fight in her.        (the lamb bleats, in the room)
GERRY: That you two still up? I've had three since midnight, and all of them backwards.
CONOR: Go to bed, Gerry.
GERRY: Go to bed yourself.

0:20  Under Conor's next transmission, far off down the track: the lambing shed. Gates crashing. Sixty ewes panicking.
CONOR: Ah, for God's sake. Would you listen to them. Something's got in with the ewes.
CONOR: Gerry, if that's your bloody dog again, I swear to God.
GERRY: Both my dogs are in. They're lying here by the fire.
CONOR: (moving now: boots in mud, rain on his hood, breathing hard) Well, something's in there. I'm going down.
MAEVE: Fox?
CONOR: If it's a fox, it's a dead fox.

0:38  A shed door dragged open. Rain on a tin roof. And no sheep. None.
CONOR: Maeve. They've gone quiet.
MAEVE: Can you see it?
CONOR: No. Sixty ewes and not a sound. They're all stood facing the back corner. Every one of them. Same corner.
GERRY: Conor. Come out of there.
CONOR: It's a fox, Gerry. I've got the torch.

0:58  Straw underfoot. His breathing. He keeps the button down.
CONOR: Right. I'm at the back pen. There's—
       (three seconds of rain)
CONOR: It's not a fox.
       (he doesn't let go of the button: rain on the roof, nothing else, four seconds; then the transmission drops)
MAEVE: Conor? ... Conor, what is it?
GERRY: Maeve. His shed lights have gone. I can see your place from here. The whole shed's gone dark.
MAEVE: Conor, answer me.

1:22  Squelch. Conor. Rain on the tin roof behind him. Very calm.
CONOR: Maeve.
MAEVE: I'm here. What's in the corner?
CONOR: Maeve.
GERRY: Maeve, there's someone crossing your yard. Under the light. Coming up to the house.
GERRY: Conor, is that you?
       (nothing on the radio; in the kitchen, the lamb, which has bleated softly under every one of Maeve's lines, stops)

1:40  Three slow knocks on the back door. In the room with us. Nothing after them.
Cut to black.

## Production notes
- Three acoustic spaces for Conor: open hay shed (wind, one ewe), the yard (boots in mud, rain on
  his hood, the distant panic), the lambing shed (tin roof, and silence). The silence is the scare.
- Conor's two calm "Maeve."s carry the tin roof behind them: he is still in the shed, 300 m away,
  when the knocks come. The knocks are dry and close, in the room; the radio light is dark.
- Kitchen: range hum, the lamb's soft bleats under Maeve; they stop before the knocks.
- Crest lessons applied: everyone on the channel hears everything; Maeve has a reason to stay (the
  lamb); Gerry can see the farm (shed lights, yard light); no jargon; no contradictions; "It's not a
  fox" is a stop, not a cut; one take per character with a long lead-in; accent-gated (CommonAccent
  'ireland').
